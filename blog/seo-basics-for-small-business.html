SEO basics for a small business website

Search engine optimisation (SEO) is the process of making a website easier for search engines to understand and more useful for people searching for relevant information. It is gradual work, not a one-time trick or a guarantee of rankings.

RIAZ IDEAS Editorial · Educational guide · Updated November 2026
In this guide: practical points you can review and apply to your own website or business.

Choose a clear topic for each page

Each important page should have a main subject. A service page should explain one service in enough detail to help a potential customer understand it. Avoid creating many near-identical pages that differ only by a location or a few words.

Write descriptive page titles

A page title should describe the page accurately and distinguish it from other pages on your site. Keep it natural and useful. Avoid repeating the same keyword unnaturally or promising something the page does not provide.

Use headings to organise information

A clear main heading tells readers what the page is about. Subheadings divide longer explanations into sections, making the page easier to scan. Headings should describe the content that follows them.

Publish genuinely helpful content

Answer real questions with original explanations, examples and steps. Add details that reflect your actual experience or research. Rewriting other websites without adding value does not create a useful resource for readers.

Connect related pages

Internal links help visitors move between related pages. For example, a guide about website planning can link to a relevant article about mobile usability. Use link text that explains what the reader will find.

Keep technical basics healthy

Use a secure HTTPS address, check that important pages load, fix broken links and make sure the site works on mobile. Submit a sitemap through Google Search Console if appropriate and review indexing reports rather than assuming every page is indexed.

Key takeaway: SEO takes time and results vary by competition, website quality and search behaviour. Measure useful outcomes—such as relevant visits and enquiries—rather than relying on a promise of a particular ranking.
