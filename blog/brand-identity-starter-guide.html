Brand identity

A beginner's guide to brand identity

Brand identity is the visual and verbal system a business uses to present itself consistently. A logo is one part of that system, but a recognisable identity also depends on colour, typography, imagery and the way the business communicates.

RIAZ IDEAS Editorial · Educational guide · Updated November 2026
In this guide: practical points you can review and apply to your own website or business.

Define the audience and personality

Before choosing colours or fonts, describe who the business serves and how it should communicate. A local repair service may need a very different tone from a creative studio or a children's learning brand.

Build a simple colour palette

Choose a primary colour, one or two supporting colours and neutral shades for backgrounds and text. Check that text remains readable against the background. Colour should help organise information, not make every element compete for attention.

Choose readable typography

A practical identity usually needs a display typeface for headings and a highly readable typeface for paragraphs. Limit the number of fonts and use consistent sizes and weights so pages feel related.

Create logo variations

Prepare versions that work on light and dark backgrounds, plus a compact version for small spaces. Test the logo at the size it will actually be used. Avoid adding tiny details that disappear on mobile screens.

Set image and layout rules

Decide what kinds of photos, illustrations, spacing and shapes fit the brand. Consistent rules make social posts, documents and web pages feel like parts of the same system.

Document the basics

Create a short brand sheet with logo files, colour values, font names and examples of correct use. This makes it easier for anyone producing future materials to keep the identity consistent.

Key takeaway: A useful brand identity is recognisable and usable. Test it on a website header, a social media post and a simple document before treating the design system as finished.
