A small business website checklist

A useful business website is not simply a collection of attractive sections. It should answer visitor questions, explain the offer and make it easy to take the next step. This checklist can help you plan the essentials before publishing.

RIAZ IDEAS Editorial · Educational guide · Updated November 2026
In this guide: practical points you can review and apply to your own website or business.

Start with one clear purpose

Write down the main job of the website. Is it meant to explain services, help people find a shop, receive enquiries, or publish educational resources? A clear primary purpose makes decisions about pages and content easier.

Create the essential pages

Most small business websites need a homepage, an about page, a page explaining products or services, and a contact page. Add other pages only when they answer a real visitor question. A privacy policy is important when the site collects information or uses advertising and analytics tools.

Write for customer questions

Explain what you offer, who it is for, where you operate if location matters, and how someone can enquire. Use specific language instead of broad claims such as 'best quality' or 'number one'. Only publish claims you can support.

Make contact simple

Show a working email address or contact method. If you use a form, tell visitors what information is needed and how it will be used. Test every link and form on a phone as well as a computer.

Check mobile usability

Open each page on a small screen. Look for text that is too small, buttons that are difficult to tap, menus that cover content and images that slow the page down. A mobile visitor should be able to find key information without zooming.

Review trust and accuracy

Use genuine business details, current prices where relevant, accurate service descriptions and original images you have permission to use. Remove placeholder text, broken links and pages that are still under construction.

Key takeaway: Before launch, ask someone unfamiliar with the business to visit the site and explain what the business does and how to contact it. If they cannot answer those questions quickly, simplify the page structure and wording.
